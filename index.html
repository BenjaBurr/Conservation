Categories of Risk

There are 7 categories of risk facing wildlife:

  • Least Concern- Species that are not currently at risk of extinction.
  • Near Threatened- Species that are close to qualifying for a threatened category in the near future.
  • Vulnerable- Species that are facing a high risk of extinction in the wild.
  • Endangered- Species that are facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild.
  • Critically Endangered- Species that are facing an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild.
  • Extinct in the Wild- Species that are known only to survive in captivity or as a naturalized population outside their historic range.
  • Extinct- Species that have no known living individuals.

Gharials

  • Gharials are a type of crocodile.
  • They have a long narrow snout, with a bobbly and bumpy nose.
  • They are found primarily in the rivers of India along the Ganges and Brahmaputra rivers.
  • They are my favourite crocodile.
  • Gharials are really cool because they are non-aggressive and play a vital role in their ecosystem.

Sea Otters

  • Sea otters are marine mammals known for their dense fur, tool use, and role as a keystone species in kelp forest ecosystems.
  • They are found along the coasts of the Pacific Ocean, from Alaska to California.
  • Sea otters are important for maintaining the health of kelp forests by preying on sea urchins, which can overgraze and damage the kelp.
  • They are adorable and have a playful nature, often seen floating on their backs and using rocks to crack open shellfish.

Asian Elephants

  • Elephants are large mammals known for their intelligence, social behavior, and distinctive features like tusks and trunks.
  • They are found in various habitats across Africa and Asia, including savannas, forests, and grasslands.
  • Elephants play a crucial role in their ecosystems by shaping habitats, dispersing seeds, and creating water holes that benefit other wildlife.
  • Asian elephants once roamed across most of Asia, now they’re restricted to just 15% of their original range, in a number of fragmented and isolated populations around south and south-east Asia.
  • Today, there are fewer than 52,000 Asian elephants left in the wild. They are also listed as endangered species on the IUCN Red List.

Tigers

  • Tigers are large cats known for their strength, agility, and distinctive orange and black stripes
  • They are found in various habitats across Africa and Asia, including savannas, forests, and grasslands.
  • Tigers are apex predators and play a crucial role in maintaining the balance of their ecosystems.
  • There are fewer than 4,000 tigers left in the wild, making them one of the most critically endangered big cats.
  • Conservation efforts are underway to protect tiger habitats and reduce poaching.
